You're asking a great question. It gets to the heart of how shell scripting languages handle data.

You are correct that gdate outputs strings. However, the printf command on the next line uses the format specifier %02d.

  • %s would mean "insert this as a string".
  • %d means "interpret this as a decimal integer and format it".

The 02 part of %02d adds a specific formatting rule: "pad with a leading zero to a width of two characters". This is what ensures that 7 becomes 07.

Because the command uses %d, bash is forced to interpret "08" and "09" as numbers before formatting them. The problem is that, by default, shells interpret numbers with a leading zero as octal (base-8). Since 8 and 9 are not valid octal digits, it fails.

So, while gdate provides a string, the *formatting requirements* of printf force a numeric interpretation, which is why we need to explicitly tell it to use base-10.

Diagnosis: the page set body text at 13px and labels at 11px. System 7 was drawn for 72 dpi screens, where Geneva 9 and Chicago 12 filled a much larger share of the display. Copying those numbers into CSS pixels shrinks the UI by roughly half. The faithful translation is to pixel-double the whole thing, the way an emulator does.

What the research says

  1. WCAG sets no minimum font size. It requires text to resize to 200% without loss of content (SC 1.4.4). Sizing text in px defeats a reader who raises the browser's default; rem does not.
  2. Content must survive a line-height of 1.5× (SC 1.4.12). The page was at 1.45.
  3. Line width should stay under 80 characters (SC 1.4.8, AAA).
  4. GOV.UK ships body text at 19px on desktop, 16px for small text (type scale). 16px is the browser default and the common floor (Accessible Web).
  5. NN/g's finding is about capability, not taste: web usability declines about 0.8% per year from age 25 (older adults).
  6. System.css, the best-known System 6/7 CSS recreation, sets its window panes, buttons, inputs and selects at 18px, titles at 1.5rem, borders at 1.5–2px. It scales the classic look up rather than reproducing bitmap sizes literally.
